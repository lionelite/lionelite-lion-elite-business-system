### 2. Run the conversation in order

| Stage | Rep action / suggested transition | Required output |
|---|---|---|
| Open | “Let's look at where you are, what you want to change, and whether we can help. Does that work?” | Agreed agenda and available time |
| Discover | Ask the selected offer's discovery questions, one at a time | Current situation, problem, desired outcome, timeline |
| Qualify | “Who else is involved in this decision?” “What would you need to see to move forward?” | Decision process, criteria, budget fit and capacity |
| Confirm the gap | “You're at [current situation], you want [goal], and [bottleneck] is getting in the way. Have I understood that correctly?” | Prospect-confirmed diagnosis |
| Present | “To address [gap], the proposed work is [deliverables], over [term], for [total price], with [responsibilities].” | Clear scope, evidence, costs, limitations |
| Resolve | Use only the branch matching the actual objection below | Answered concern or explicit outstanding question |
| Agree next step | “Based on what we've covered, does [specific next step] make sense?” | Action, owner, date/time and timezone |
| Record and hand off | Complete the private call record and assign the next task | No active opportunity without an owner and due date |

If there is no fit, record why and close respectfully. Do not run all three objection scripts on every prospect.

### 3. Route objections to the right script

| What the prospect says | Use | Return point |
|---|---|---|

## Master sales flow

Open → discover current situation → establish desired outcome → diagnose the gap → explain the offer → present evidence and price → handle objections → agree on the next step.

Ask one question at a time. Use the prospect’s actual answers in follow-up questions. The objection sequence follows the supplied video: permission, credibility, identity, buy-in, reinforcement, selection, third-person consequence, tie-down, personal consequence, clarification, commitment.

### Objection handling — credibility, experience, or skepticism

1. **Permission:** “Can I be real with you?”
2. **Credibility:** “When you evaluate a marketing partner, how much weight do you put on years in business versus evidence that they can solve your specific problem?”
3. **Identity:** “You’ve said you want to grow beyond [current revenue] and build toward [target]. That’s why we’re discussing what needs to change.”
4. **Buy-in:** “Would you agree that the process, relevant results, and ability to execute are what we need to examine?”
5. **Reinforcement:** “Being busy marketing for years doesn’t necessarily mean there’s a repeatable way to acquire profitable clients.”
6. **Selection:** “Would you rather keep repeating your current approach, or evaluate a measurable process designed around the gap we identified?”
7. **Third-person consequence:** “What tends to happen when a business keeps losing interested prospects because nobody follows up consistently?”
8. **Tie-down:** “Is that the pattern you want to continue?”
9. **Personal consequence:** “If your revenue stayed around [current amount] for another year, what would that mean for you?”
10. **Clarify:** “When you say [their words], what does that look like in practice?”
11. **Commitment:** “Based on the scope, evidence, and investment we’ve reviewed, are you ready to move forward with [specific agreed next step]?”

## Lion Elite Clinical — Business buyer script

### Opening

“Hi [name], this is [rep] with Lion Elite Clinical. Who handles supplier evaluation and purchasing for your practice?”

With the decision-maker:

“We’d like to understand your purchasing requirements and see whether there’s a fit on documentation, reliability, and total cost. What would a new supplier have to demonstrate for you to consider them?”

### Discovery and offer

“What are you currently purchasing, in what volume, and how frequently?”

“What matters most in your evaluation?”

“Where is your current arrangement falling short, if anywhere?”

“Who else reviews and approves a new supplier?”

“Based on those requirements, the next step would be a documented comparison covering [verified offering], specifications, applicable sourcing information, pricing, testing requirements, and written terms.”

Use only verified product, supplier, and regulatory information. Do not describe research-use-only products as products for patient treatment or claim 503B status without verification.

### Objection handling — “We already have a supplier” or “Why trust you?”

1. **Permission:** “Can I offer another way to evaluate it?”
2. **Credibility:** “Would it be useful to judge us against your written requirements and supporting documentation?”
3. **Identity:** “You’re responsible for protecting the practice while making sound purchasing decisions.”
4. **Buy-in:** “Would you agree that quality requirements, reliability, and total cost all matter?”
5. **Reinforcement:** “A longstanding relationship is valuable. A documented comparison gives you a basis for deciding whether your current arrangement remains the best fit.”
6. **Selection:** “Would you be open to comparing a qualified alternative before deciding whether any change makes sense?”
7. **Third-person consequence:** “What happens to a practice that never reviews recurring supplier issues or purchasing costs?”
8. **Tie-down:** “Is that something you want to avoid?”
9. **Personal consequence:** “How is [the issue they identified] affecting your practice today?”
10. **Clarify:** “Can you give me a recent example—and what it cost in time or money?”
11. **Commitment:** “Let’s schedule a requirements review with the people who approve purchasing. What time works?”

## Shared execution rules

- Pause after questions; respond to the answer instead of mechanically running every step.
- Support credibility with relevant, verifiable evidence.
- Address genuine concerns about price, suitability, or missing evidence directly.
- Use the prospect’s own stated consequences; do not invent personal fears.
- Record the objection, underlying concern, evidence provided, response, and agreed next step.
- Track booked meetings, held meetings, qualified opportunities, proposals, wins, and collected revenue separately.
- End with a specific next action, owner, and date. Respect a clear decline.

## Reference — Video-matched age objection

Reconstructed from on-screen captions; not a verbatim transcript.

**Objection:** “You’re younger than me. I’ve been doing this longer than you’ve been alive. How can you help me?”

1. **Permission to reframe:** “Can I be real with you?” Pause for their answer.
2. **Reframe credibility:** “What does it matter how long someone’s been in sales if the results aren’t where they want them to be?”
3. **Identity frame:** “You seem like someone who wants to grow. You have ambition, and you want to get somewhere—that’s why we’re having this conversation.”
4. **Get buy-in:** “What matters more: how long somebody’s been in sales, or how successful they’ve actually been?” Let them answer.
5. **Reinforce the frame:** “Exactly. Someone could spend 40 years doing something without getting the results you want. The number of years alone doesn’t tell you whether their approach is worth following.”
6. **Reframe mentor selection:** “If the purpose of having a mentor is to learn from their knowledge and replicate their success, whose approach would you rather learn? Someone who took 20 years to reach a certain level, or someone who reached that same level in a much shorter time?”
7. **Third-person consequence:** “What do you think happens to someone who chooses a mentor purely because they’ve been doing it longer, without looking at the results or how they achieved them?” Pause. “Could they end up taking that same long road?”
8. **Tie-down:** “Is that what you want?”
9. **Personal consequence:** “What would it mean for you if reaching your goal took another 20 years?” Let them explain. “What would that cost you personally?”
10. **Clarify and probe:** “What do you mean by that?” “How is that affecting you now?” Follow their actual answer. In the video, the prospect raises concerns about his marriage and arguments over money; only explore those topics if the prospect brings them up.
11. **Commitment question:** “So, coming back to what you said you want: whose approach makes more sense to learn from—the person who took decades, or the person who achieved the result faster?” Pause. “Then does it make sense for us to move forward?”


## Sales 2 — Price-to-value objection handling

Added November 5, 2026. Source: supplied “Sales 2.mp4” (approximately 104 seconds). The sequence below follows the visible instructional labels and captions. Dialogue is adapted for the BuildPipeline marketing offer, not a verbatim audio transcript. Revised to the selected closer-to-video wording.

### Video sequence and placement

Use after presenting the actual scope and price, when the prospect says “That’s a lot” or “That’s too expensive.” For age/credibility objections, use the earlier framework instead.

| Step | Video label | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pattern interrupt & clarify | Find out what “expensive” means. |
| 2 | Shift cost based thinking to value based thinking | Compare the price with what is being purchased. |
| 3 | Get buy in | Confirm that value depends on the offer, not the number alone. |
| 4 | Identity frame | Connect the decision to the prospect’s stated priorities. |
| 5 | Belief in solution? | Check whether they believe the proposed approach fits. |
| 6 | Reframe the $15k | Revisit the quoted price in context; $15,000 belongs to the video example. |
| 7 | Isolate value driver | Ask which specific component matters most. |
| 8 | Self persuasion | Let the prospect explain why that component matters. |
| 9 | Probe | Explore their answer rather than supplying it. |
| 10 | Identity alignment question | Relate the decision to their goal and decision criteria. |
| 11 | Commitment question | Ask for a clear, appropriate next step. |

The clip uses a Ferrari price analogy to show that a number alone does not establish value. The analogy illustrates perceived value; it does not establish the value or likely return of the actual offer. The price, income figures, and outcomes in the clip are not evidence for any Lion Elite or BuildPipeline claim.

### BuildPipeline — Video-style price objection

Use this version after presenting the actual offer and price. This is the selected Sales 2 script; the previous separate Clinical, Beauty, and Wellness price-objection adaptations are superseded by this single version. Their earlier discovery and credibility scripts remain separate.

**Prospect:** “That’s a lot of money.”

**1. Pattern interrupt and clarify**

“A lot?”

*Pause.*

“What do you mean by a lot?”

**2. Shift cost to value**

“Would you pay $10,000 for a Ferrari?”

*Let them answer.*

“So $10,000 isn’t automatically expensive. It depends on what you’re getting for it.”

**3. Get buy-in**

“If you felt what you were getting was worth more than what you were paying, would you still call it expensive?”

*Pause.*

**4. Identity frame**

“Exactly. As a business owner, the question becomes: is it worth it, or is it not worth it?”

**5. Belief in the solution**

“Based on everything we covered, do you feel this could help you get from [current revenue] toward [their $35K–$50K monthly goal]?”

*Wait for their answer.*

**6. Reframe the investment**

“So is $10,000 too much—or do you need to feel more certain that this can help you get there?”

*Resolve any uncertainty before continuing.*

**7. Isolate the value driver**

“What specifically do you feel would help you get there?”

*Let them name it.*

**8. Self-persuasion**

“Why that?”

*Pause.*

“And what would that allow you to do?”

**9. Probe**

“And what would that mean for you?”

*Let them explain.*

**10. Identity alignment**

“So, thinking as the business owner you’re working toward becoming, how would you evaluate this decision?”

*Pause.*

“Would this be a worthwhile investment based on what we’ve covered?”

**11. Commitment question**

“Then what do you feel is the logical thing for you to do next?”

*Let them answer.*

“Should we get you started?”

**Economics check:** Use contribution after variable delivery costs, not revenue alone, when discussing break-even. Illustrative only: the $10,000 offer fee divided by $1,000 contribution per incremental client requires ten incremental clients to cover the fee alone, before any additional advertising, software or other overhead. Use a matching time period, include recurring costs, and do not imply those clients are guaranteed. A $35K–$50K revenue goal is not a return forecast.

### Branching and outcome recording

- If the issue is **affordability**, acknowledge it. Discuss only real, available options or an agreed later review; do not pressure the prospect into debt.
- If the issue is **belief or missing proof**, return to the unanswered question and provide relevant evidence. Do not advance to commitment while the concern is unresolved.
- If the issue is **comparison**, compare equivalent scope and total costs; acknowledge when the alternative better fits.
- If the issue is **value**, ask the prospect to identify the useful component and explain its importance in their own words.
- If the prospect declines, respect the decision.
- Record the exact objection, category, value driver, evidence requested/provided, unresolved concern, and next action with owner/date.

## Sales 3 — Resistance to discovery questions

Added November 5, 2026. Source: supplied “Sales 3.mp4” (76 seconds). Reconstructed from its visible captions and seven instructional labels; this is a close adaptation, not a verified verbatim audio transcript. Kept as one BuildPipeline script.

**Placement:** Use during discovery when a prospect treats the questions as unnecessary or a waste of time. This is a conversation reset, not a price objection or closing sequence.

**Prospect objection, paraphrased:** “Can you just get to the point? These questions are unnecessary. I don’t want to waste time.”

### 1. Pattern interrupt

“Hold on—we’ve only just started the call, and I think we need to reset the dynamic of this conversation.”

*Pause. Stay composed.*

### 2. Reframe answering questions from annoying to productive

“The reason I’m asking is to understand whether we can help you improve your sales process, conversion rate, and profitability. If we don’t understand what’s happening in your business, how can we figure out what actually needs to change?”

### 3. Takeaway / pullback

“If that isn’t a conversation you want to have, we can end the call here. I don’t want to waste your time or mine.”

*Make this a genuine option. If they want to stop, stop.*

### 4. Identity frame

“You seem like someone who wants to grow and take care of your business. But I can’t want that for you more than you want it for yourself.”

*Use only goals the prospect has actually expressed. Deliver calmly, not as a challenge to their worth.*

### 5. Requalify

“If you want to have a productive conversation about what you need, so we can figure out whether we can even help and put a plan together, let’s do that.”

“But if you don’t want to have that conversation, that’s okay too. What would you prefer?”

*Wait for their answer.*

**If the prospect says previous sales calls wasted their time:** Continue to step 6.

**If they agree to proceed without raising past experiences:** Go directly to step 7.

### 6. Shift focus from past to present

“I understand that. But the people you’ve spoken with before, where you felt they wasted your time—were they me?”

*Pause.*

“We’ve only just started speaking. You don’t really know me yet, and I don’t know enough about your business yet. Can we judge this conversation on what we actually do here?”

### 7. Transition back to process

“I’ll keep it direct. I still need to understand enough about your situation to work out whether we can even help you. I’m not here to waste your time—that’s not my intention. Fair?”

*Wait for agreement, then return to the last unanswered discovery question.*

**BuildPipeline return-to-discovery line:**

“Where are most of your clients coming from right now, and where do you feel the sales process is breaking down?”

### Rep implementation

- Use the reset once, then listen. Do not repeatedly challenge the prospect.
- If the issue is limited time, agree on a shorter agenda or reschedule.
- If they ask a direct factual question about price or scope, answer what is known; do not use discovery as a way to evade it.
- State the purpose of the next question and ask only what is relevant.
- If they decline, end respectfully. Record the reason and any explicitly agreed follow-up.
- Record whether the reset led to resumed discovery, rescheduling, or a declined conversation.
- This implements Sales 3 in the sales playbook; it does not change live software, launch outreach, or promise conversion improvements.
